Cars

Car ownership reflects a country's wealth. In rich nations, almost every adult owns a car, while developing countries have far fewer. As middle classes grow in China and India, ownership is rising fast. In Scandinavia, electric vehicles dominate, driven by high incomes and strong environmental policy. Vehicle density is a direct mirror of a nation's economic development.

Mongolia

Mongolia has just 3.3 million people across 1.5 million square kilometers, making it one of the world's least dense countries. Nearly half live in Ulaanbaatar. Younger generations are leaving nomadic life for cities in search of work and education. With only 2 people per square kilometer, Mongolia's vast emptiness defines its entire demographic identity.

Slum

Over a billion people live in slums, mostly where GDP per capita falls below $2,000. Cities grow faster than infrastructure follows. GDP is the strongest predictor of slum prevalence. Education and employment are the keys to reducing poverty.

Population

The world hit 8 billion in 2022. Growth concentrates in Africa and South Asia while Europe declines. Dense nations struggle with housing and food. Fertility, life expectancy, and migration are the three drivers of population change.

Hands

A young population fuels growth through labor. Aging societies like Japan face shortages and pension strain. Migration fills the gap. When women enter the workforce, GDP rises. Demographics shape every nation's working hands.
